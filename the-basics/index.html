Where to buy a Laptop.

There are many ways to buy your laptop. I classify them into 2 types

  1. Used Laptops
  2. Brand New Laptops.

 
Now, When it comes to buying New it's pretty straightforward. You basically walk-in to a store and buy your already researched and decided upon Laptop or you order your decided Laptop from your respective Online Store. But almost always Brand new Laptops are expensive than the used counterparts.

Moving on to the Used market scene of laptops. Most of the time buying used Laptops are kind of a gamble. But, In my experience used laptops from large organisations(Like, Governments, NGO, Corporations etc.) tend to be well taken care of and are almost always in better shape than the normie used Laptop counterparts.

Now It's completely up to you. If you consider used laptops then you can easily save a lot of money and put it towards a better specced laptop or other things. But if you go the brand new route you can easily get services and also can live with the relief of the laptop being free of any aliments.

What laptop to choose.

There are many classes of laptops out there. But I classify them into the following,

  1. Desktop Replacements
  2. Mobile Workstations
  3. Thin and Light workstations
  4. Gaming Laptops
  5. UltraBooks (& Convertibles)
  6. Everyday

When it comes to choosing the class of laptops. It depends on your use case and budget.For most people Everyday class is very much suitable. But to those who have much deeper pockets, prefer a good design and are ready to sacrifice battery life(in some rare cases they don't have to) they can choose the ultrabooks. Next up Gamers obviously will and have to choose the Gaming Laptops(not all who choose the gaming class of laptops are gamers some of them are developers and video editors alike.) And those who are in the professional class of life like engineers, data-analysts, VFX artists, 3D-artists, and video editors will have to most likely choose the Mobile Workstation class or the Desktop replacement class.

OS 

Now, For most people the OS of their choice is still going to be Windows(Although, some are dabbling in Linux). So they have many options. Mostly the similar market advantage applies to those who want to daily-drive or use Linux as their main OS.

Now, A marginal percentage of the readers use macOS as their gateway to the digital life. Many people can get by with MacBooks and macOS. If they don't use Strictly non- macOS apps.

 

Anyway guys, these are the basics of the guide. More guidance regarding what specs to look for when choosing the laptops will be published very soon. And make sure to choose  which class of laptop you need based on the above paragraphs.
